The Effects of Acquisitions by Digital Platforms on Innovation

July 2025, Working Paper | Abstract | PDF

Abstract: This study examines the effects of acquisitions by digital platforms on innovation activity. Using the number of patents granted to a company as a proxy for innovation, I study acquisitions by digital platforms of firms operating in the Russian market, including startups. I estimate dynamic effects using the Dynamic Difference-in-Differences approach of Imai, Kim, and Wang (2023). The results show a short-term negative effect of digital-platform acquisitions on innovation two months after the transaction, while no such effect is observed for startup acquisitions. Acquisitions by digital ecosystems have a positive effect after eleven months, whereas acquisitions by other digital platforms show a short-term positive effect.

Financial Literacy and Responsible Financial Behaviour of Russian Households

with Andrey Sinyakov and Tatyana Shelovanova

Bank of Russia Working Paper No. 132, 2024 | SSRN

Abstract: Using the 2020 and 2022 waves of the All-Russian Survey of Consumer Finances, we study the relationship between financial literacy and responsible financial behaviour. The analysis considers consumption smoothing, asset diversification, investment choices, debt burden, information acquisition, perceptions of macroeconomic trends, and confidence about the future. We find a positive relationship between financial literacy and overall responsible financial behaviour, with particularly important links to consumption smoothing and diversification of savings.

Research in progress

Foreign-Agent Designations and Political Speech in Russia

Independent research project

Abstract: This project studies how highly visible political repression changes the public positions of targeted individuals and organizations. Using Russia's foreign-agent designations and online speech, I examine whether stigmatizing legal labels deter dissent or instead generate countervailing effects through visibility, recognition, and credibility among regime opponents.

Missing Markets and the Boundaries of the Firm

Ongoing research

Abstract: Why do firms in lower-income economies internalize activities that similar firms in richer economies outsource? This project studies how thin supplier markets, contracting and enforcement frictions, and missing infrastructure shape firms' choices between producing tasks in-house, outsourcing them, or dropping them altogether.

Talent Discovery and Hiring Practices

Ongoing research

Abstract: This project examines why firms use different approaches to discovering talent: intensive screening before hire versus broader hiring followed by learning on the job. The goal is to measure firm-specific hiring practices in worker career data and study when talent discovery can become a source of competitive advantage.
